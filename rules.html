The TLPT Floor Book

House Rules

The rulings that keep the game moving, the action clear, and the post-hand litigation to a respectable minimum.

The order in the room

Clear action. Protected cards. One final ruling.

These are TLPT house rules. If an edge case is not covered below, pause the action and call the host. The fairest ruling available at the table is final for the night.

View Blind Structures

Money & Entry

1

Rebuy / Reload

Rebuys and reloads follow the posted blind structure for that event. A reload is available only while the window is open and your stack is at or below 20% of the starting stack. Once the window closes, the cashier has left the building.

2

Late Arrivals

Tell the host before the event begins if you will be late. Your stack and blind treatment follow the announced event setup; showing up with coffee does not rewind the clock.

3

Payouts & Chops

The posted payout schedule governs the event. Any chop requires every remaining player to agree; otherwise, play continues and the cards settle it like civilized people.

4

Bounties

When an event includes paid bounties, they are tracked separately from tournament profit. Every elimination is still a knockout; only qualifying eliminations also earn bounty credit.

Action & Betting

5

Declarations Are Binding

A clear verbal declaration made in turn is binding. Announce calls, raises, and all-ins before moving chips when possible. One player to a hand applies, even when the gallery has notes.

6

No String Bets

Put the full wager forward in one motion or announce the amount first. Returning to your stack for a dramatic sequel does not make it a bigger bet.

7

Action Out of Turn

Acting out of turn may be binding if the action before you does not change. Stop and call the host before anyone adds more chips; table speed is not a substitute for table order.

8

Raises & Short All-Ins

A raise must meet the full minimum raise. A short all-in does not reopen betting to players who already acted unless the action reaches a full raise; the host settles cumulative-action edge cases.

9

Calling the Clock

Players deserve reasonable time for a real decision. After that, any player may request a clock; the host decides when the countdown begins and when the hand is dead.

Cards & the Deal

10

Protect Live Cards

Keep your hand visible and protected. If unprotected cards are pulled into the muck and cannot be identified with certainty, they may be dead. The Commish has card protectors for a reason. #YouveBeenWarned

11

Exposed Cards

Do not turn an accidental exposure into a self-declared misdeal. Freeze the action, leave the card where it is, and let the host determine the remedy.

12

Misdeals

Stop the hand as soon as a possible misdeal is noticed. Once substantial action has occurred, the hand normally continues and the host applies the fairest available correction.

13

Showdown

Table all cards face up to claim a pot. Cards speak, but players remain responsible for holding onto a winning hand until the pot is awarded.

14

Odd Chips

Unavoidable odd chips in a split pot follow the room’s button-order procedure. If a chip cannot be split, the host awards it consistently—not by arm wrestling.

Table Management

15

Heads-Up Order

Heads up, the button posts the small blind, acts first before the flop, and acts last after the flop. Yes, it feels backward right up until it does not.

16

Balancing & the Merge

For multi-table events, the host balances tables and assigns moves. The field merges to one table at nine players unless the event page announces otherwise.

17

Players at the Table

Keep chips visible, larger denominations out front, and commentary away from live decisions. Advice, coded sighs, and unsolicited coaching all count as helping.

18

Floor Authority

The host may use fairness and the best interest of the game when a situation is not covered exactly. The ruling is final for the event; appeals may be filed with the complaint department, which is the next group chat.
